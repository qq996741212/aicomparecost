Best AI Email Assistants: Which One Actually Saves You Hours a Week

2026-09-26 · Alex

Here's the verdict up front: an AI email assistant saves you real time, but almost nobody needs the most expensive option. Most people are drowning in email not because they type too slowly, but because they write too much and think too little about what actually needs a reply. The tools that help most are the ones that force you to be brief — not the ones that generate a longer, more "professional-sounding" version of the same rambling message.

I've spent the last several weeks running a handful of these tools across two mailboxes — a personal Gmail that gets the usual flood, and a work inbox that gets the worse flood. Here's what actually held up.

The Tools I Tested

I kept the list short and realistic. There are dozens of these, but most are variations on five ideas:

  • Superhuman — a premium email client that rebuilds the whole inbox around

speed, with AI reply and summarization built in.

  • Shortwave — a Gmail-native AI client that bundles, labels, and drafts

for you, with a genuinely useful free tier.

  • Lavender — a sales-email coach that scores your drafts and tells you

what to cut before you hit send.

  • Grammarly — the writing assistant everyone already knows, now with

tone and conciseness suggestions aimed at email.

  • A plain chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) — the zero-setup option where

you paste a request and paste back the reply.

Where Each One Shines

Superhuman: speed, not writing quality

Superhuman's pitch has never really been "write better emails" — it's "get through your inbox in half the time." The keyboard-first interface genuinely is fast once the muscle memory sets in, and the AI reply feature is a nice touch: it drafts a short, polite response you can accept with one keystroke.

The catch is the price. At around thirty bucks a month, you're paying a lot for the interface, not the intelligence. If you already have a system that works and you just want help composing, this is overkill.

Shortwave: the best "set it and forget it" option

Shortwave is the one I'd point a normal, busy person to first. It sits on top of Gmail, auto-bundles your newsletters and receipts so they stop polluting your inbox, and its AI features — instant summaries of long threads, one-click drafts, reminders — are genuinely useful without being gimmicky.

The free tier is enough to feel the difference, which is rare in this category. The paid tier mainly removes limits and adds more advanced features like scheduling follow-ups. It's the closest thing to "AI email for everyone" I found.

Lavender: for people who sell over email

Lavender is a specific tool for a specific problem: writing emails that get replies from people who don't know you. It scores your draft in real time, flags sentences that are too long, suggests a shorter subject line, and shows you the person's recent activity to help you personalize.

If you do cold outreach or sales follow-ups for a living, this pays for itself fast. If you don't, it's pointless — a wrench when you need a butter knife.

Grammarly: the quiet safety net

Grammarly won't reorganize your inbox or score your funnel, but its email suggestions are the most invisible and reliable of the bunch. It catches the tone slip in a reply you fired off while annoyed, tightens wordy sentences, and fixes grammar without you thinking about it.

The new conciseness feature is the part I actually like: it flags the exact phrase that's doing no work and tells you to delete it. For most people, that single feature is worth more than any AI draft generator.

A plain chatbot: better than you'd expect

Don't sleep on the boring option. Pasting "turn this into a short, friendly reply that says no without sounding rude" into ChatGPT or Claude works shockingly well, and you probably already have access to it. The downside is friction — copy, switch tabs, paste, copy back — and the fact that it has zero context about the thread, the people, or your history.

It's the right answer for occasional use and the wrong answer for all-day email work.

The One Thing That Mattered More Than the Tool

I want to flag the real finding here, because it's not about any of the above products. The single biggest time sink in email is replies you don't need to write.

Roughly a third of what hit my inbox either needed no reply, needed a one-liner, or was someone asking me to do something a quick search could answer. No AI tool fixes that. The fix is discipline: if a reply is shorter than two sentences, the "smart reply" button is already good enough. Save the AI for the messages where tone or stakes actually matter.

What Didn't Work

Expecting AI to make cold email feel warm. Lavender and every chatbot can help you sound less robotic, but if the underlying message is generic, the AI just produces polished generic. No tool turns a bad pitch into a good one.

Letting Superhuman's speed features go to waste. If you don't commit to the keyboard shortcuts, you've paid a premium for a prettier Gmail. It's an all-in or nothing tool, and I'd bet most subscribers use 20% of it.

Relying on AI summaries for anything legal or financial. Summaries are great for "what's this 14-message thread about," but I caught a couple of instances where a model flattened a nuance that actually mattered — a deadline, a caveat, a "but." Read the important ones yourself.

Trying to fix inbox overload with more software. I added tools, and my inbox still got full — because the problem was my triage habits, not my typing. The software helps after you've decided to stop treating every email like a crisis.

Verdict

  • Most people: start with Shortwave's free tier (or just Grammarly if

you're happy with your inbox and only want better writing). You'll feel the win immediately, without a subscription.

  • Sales and outreach: Lavender is the clear pick — it's built for

exactly your job.

  • Power users who live in email: Superhuman earns its keep, but only

if you actually adopt the workflow.

  • Everyone else: a plain chatbot is free and covers 80% of what you

need, if you can live with the copy-paste friction.

The honest summary: the tool matters less than you think, and the habit of replying shorter, replying less, and reading what's actually important matters more than any of them. Pick the cheapest option that removes your biggest specific annoyance, then move on with your life.

FAQ

Which AI email assistant is the best free option? Shortwave's free tier is the strongest free pick because it does real inbox triage in addition to AI drafting. If you only care about writing quality, Grammarly's free tier plus a free chatbot covers most of it.

Does Superhuman actually make you faster? For people who commit to the keyboard-first workflow, yes — the speed gains are real. But it's an all-in tool, and if you use it like normal Gmail, you're paying a premium for very little.

Can I just use ChatGPT for my email? Yes, and for occasional drafting it's a perfectly good free option. The trade-off is friction (copy-paste) and no context about your threads or contacts. For all-day email work, a native assistant saves more time.

Are AI email summaries reliable enough to trust? For quick orientation on long threads, generally yes. But don't rely on them for anything with legal, financial, or deadline-sensitive nuance — read those yourself, because summaries can flatten exactly the detail that matters.
