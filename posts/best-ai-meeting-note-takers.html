Best AI Meeting Note-Takers in 2026: I Tested Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, and tl;dv

2026-09-23 · Alex

Here's the short version: if you just want a transcript and a summary that shows up in your inbox ten minutes after the call ends, the free tier of Fathom or tl;dv is genuinely enough for most people. You only need to pay once you care about searchable archives, CRM sync, or the kind of notes that read like a human wrote them — and even then, the tool that's right for you depends more on which meeting platform you live in than on raw feature lists.

I've spent the last month running four of the big names — Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, and tl;dv — on my real calendar: client calls, internal standups, sales demos, and a couple of job interviews I was on the other side of. This is what I'd tell a friend, not a spec sheet.

What Actually Matters (And What Doesn't)

Before I get into each tool, here's the thing I wish someone had told me: transcript accuracy is a solved problem. All four of these get the words right often enough that you'll stop checking. The real differences show up in three places nobody screenshots:

  1. **Does the summary tell you what to do?** A wall of transcript text is

useless. Action items with owners, plus a crisp "what happened" paragraph, is where a tool either earns its keep or wastes your time.

  1. **Does it work with your meeting platform?** If you live in Microsoft

Teams, a Zoom-native tool is going to fight you. This matters more than any benchmark.

  1. Can you find something from three weeks ago? Search and library

features are the difference between "cute toy" and "system of record."

The Tools

Fathom — the free tier that feels illegal

Fathom has been my default for a reason: the free tier is effectively unlimited, the summaries are genuinely good, and the little recap that pops up at the end of a call — with action items, a highlight reel, and a "next steps" list — is better than what some paid tools ship.

The catch: it's built for Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, and it shows. If you're on some other platform or recording a lot of phone calls, you'll feel the seams. There's also no real mobile story, so don't expect it to replace a voice recorder app.

Best for: people on Zoom/Meet/Teams who want great summaries for free.

tl;dv — the power user's library

tl;dv's killer feature is the timestamped clip. When a client says something important at the 14:20 mark, you don't send them a 45-minute recording — you send a 20-second clip. It's also the best of the bunch at search: transcripts, speakers, and even keywords inside the video are all searchable later.

The downside is polish. The interface is more "engineer's toolbox" than "clean product," and the free tier, while generous on recordings, nudges you toward paid plans for the collaboration features that actually matter in a team.

Best for: sales teams and anyone who shares clips and needs deep search.

Otter — the most accurate, the most familiar

Otter is still the name most people know, and for good reason. Its transcription accuracy is marginally the best of the four, and it has the only mobile app that's actually pleasant to use — record an in-person meeting and it'll do a decent job.

But Otter is also the one that feels most like it's squeezing you toward the subscription. The free tier has a real monthly cap, and the pricing has crept up over the years. You're paying partly for the brand.

Best for: mobile-heavy use, in-person meetings, and people who want the highest raw accuracy.

Fireflies — the automation engine

Fireflies is the one to pick if you already live in a CRM. The hook is automation: it'll push meeting summaries into Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, or whatever your team runs on, and it has the broadest integration list of the group. If your job is "make the CRM update itself," this is your tool.

If you're not on a CRM, though, a lot of that value evaporates, and you're left with a solid-but-unremarkable notetaker paying for features you won't touch.

Best for: sales/ops people who need meeting data flowing into other systems automatically.

What Didn't Work

Expecting any of them to be a magic "do my follow-ups" button. The summaries are good, but they summarize the meeting, not the work. I still had to actually do the follow-ups. The tool saved me note-taking time, not execution time.

Using a Zoom-native tool for in-person stuff. I tried to force Fathom into a couple of in-person workshops with a laptop mic and it was a mess. These tools are built for the virtual-call world; for real rooms, Otter's phone app is the only one that held up.

Ignoring the platform question. I spent two weeks mildly annoyed that my tool "wasn't catching everything" before realizing I was on a platform it didn't play nicely with. Match the tool to where your meetings actually happen, and most of my complaints would never have happened.

Chasing the transcript word-for-word. At some point I realized I was reading transcripts instead of the summaries. The transcript is the source, not the deliverable. If you find yourself re-reading every line, you're defeating the whole purpose.

Verdict

If you're on Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams and want great summaries without paying — start with Fathom. The free tier is the best of the bunch, and most people will never actually need the paid plan.

If you send clips or need to search weeks of meetings — get tl;dv. The timestamped-clip workflow alone is worth it for sales and client work.

If you live in a CRM and want the data to move itself — get Fireflies.

If you need a real mobile app or the best raw accuracy — Otter is still the safe, familiar default, just know you'll pay for it.

My honest setup: Fathom as the daily driver because it's free and good, and I'll only switch to tl;dv or Fireflies if the clip-sharing or CRM stuff becomes a real part of my workflow. Don't overthink it — pick the one that fits your platform, and stop reading transcripts like they're the deliverable.

FAQ

Are AI meeting note-takers actually accurate? For the most part, yes — transcript accuracy has gotten good enough that you'll rarely need to correct it. The bigger variable is summary quality and whether the tool fits your meeting platform, not word error rate.

Is the free tier really enough? For a single person who just wants transcripts and summaries, usually yes — Fathom and tl;dv both have free tiers that are genuinely usable. You start paying when you need search across many meetings, team collaboration, or CRM integrations.

Will my meeting participants know it's recording? Yes — and that's a feature, not a bug. The bot joins visibly and announces itself, which keeps you on the right side of consent laws in most places. Always check your local and company rules before recording.

Which one works best for in-person meetings? Otter, because of its mobile app. The Zoom-native tools assume you're on a virtual call, and they struggle in a physical room with a laptop mic.
